## From the Trenches

As a cybersecurity practitioner, I'm seeing a rise in attacks targeting critical infrastructure. The recent attacks on grocery and medicine refrigeration systems, for example, are concerning. According to reports, hackers are exploiting vulnerabilities in these systems, putting public health at risk. This highlights the need for organizations to prioritize vulnerability management and ensure that their systems are up-to-date with the latest security patches.

Another trend I'm observing is the increasing use of AI-powered attacks. The fact that antiFox AI pentesting platform was linked to attacks that accessed AWS production secrets is a stark reminder of the risks associated with using these tools. As we move forward, it's essential for organizations to understand the implications of using AI-powered tools and take steps to mitigate potential security risks.

The latest Microsoft patch release includes 1,259 flaws, including an exploited Outlook bug that may expose NTLMv2 hashes. This is a clear indication that attackers are targeting vulnerabilities in widely used applications. As a practitioner, I would prioritize patching this vulnerability ASAP, especially for organizations using Outlook.

**🔧 Patch Priority:** Cisco BroadWorks flaw, as it has already been actively exploited in attacks and could have significant consequences for organizations relying on this product.

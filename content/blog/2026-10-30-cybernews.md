## From the Trenches

As a cybersecurity practitioner, I've been keeping an eye on some concerning trends in the past few days. The first story that caught my attention is the exploitation of VMware ESX zero-day by China-linked hackers to reduce audit trail visibility. This is a serious issue because it allows attackers to cover their tracks and make it much harder for security teams to detect and respond to incidents. In my experience, reducing audit trail visibility can be a game-changer for attackers, making it easier for them to stay under the radar.

Another story that stands out is the exploitation of PHP2Shell flaws in internet-exposed Palo Alto Networks management interfaces by attackers. This highlights the importance of keeping management interfaces secure and up-to-date. In my experience, many organizations neglect to patch their management interfaces, leaving them vulnerable to exploitation. It's a reminder that security is not just about protecting user-facing applications, but also about securing all aspects of an organization's IT infrastructure.

As I'm sure many of you are aware, the number of ransomware attacks has been on the rise in recent months. According to some reports, this is due to the emergence of new attack vectors and the increasing availability of ransomware tools. As a practitioner, I've seen firsthand how devastating these types of attacks can be for organizations. It's essential that we all take steps to prevent these types of incidents, whether it's through regular backups, robust security measures, or simply being vigilant about suspicious activity.

**🔧 Patch Priority:** Palo Alto Networks must prioritize patching their management interfaces ASAP to prevent further exploitation of PHP2Shell flaws.

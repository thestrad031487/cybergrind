## From the Trenches

As a cybersecurity practitioner, I'm seeing more and more threats emerging that can be easily exploited by attackers. One of the most concerning stories this week is the potential for AI to hijack smart home devices like refrigerators and vacuum cleaners (Newsonjapan.com). This is not just a theoretical risk - we've already seen instances where attackers have compromised IoT devices to spread malware or gain access to sensitive information. The fact that these devices are increasingly connected to the internet makes them vulnerable to exploitation, and it's only a matter of time before we see this scenario play out in real-world attacks.

Another story that caught my attention is the use of AI agents as a phishing tactic (Futurism). Attackers are using AI-powered tools to create highly convincing emails that can trick users into divulging sensitive information or clicking on malicious links. This is a classic example of social engineering, but with an AI twist that makes it even harder to detect. As practitioners, we need to be aware of these tactics and develop strategies to educate our users about the risks.

The recent announcements from Trend Micro and ConnectWise are also worth noting. Trend Micro's ¥10 billion investment in its Cyber Resilience Initiative is a significant step towards strengthening security for Japanese organizations in the AI era (PRNewswire). Meanwhile, ConnectWise has reduced cybersecurity risk for its customers by implementing the Tenable One Exposure Management Platform (Tenable.com). These developments demonstrate the importance of proactive security measures and the need for organizations to prioritize their cybersecurity posture.

**🔧 Patch Priority:** Dell's NetScaler product needs immediate attention due to the critical RCE vulnerability CVE-2026-88771 (Zscaler.com), which can be exploited by attackers to gain unauthorized access to systems.

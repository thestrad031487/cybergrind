## From the Trenches

As a cybersecurity practitioner, I've seen my fair share of breaches and vulnerabilities, but some trends are concerning nonetheless. Take, for instance, the fact that security attention fades within months after a breach (18.133). This is a classic case of the "breach response fatigue" phenomenon, where organizations rush to patch vulnerabilities only to lose momentum soon after. It's crucial for companies to maintain a proactive approach to cybersecurity, rather than just reacting to incidents.

Another story that caught my attention is the one about AWS putting AI vulnerability detection to the test, but encountering false positives (Help Net Security). While AI-driven security solutions show promise, they're not without their flaws. As we move forward with increased adoption of AI in cybersecurity, it's essential to develop more robust testing protocols to avoid unnecessary alerts and false alarms.

As a practitioner, I've seen firsthand how quickly malware can spread through vulnerable systems. The recent exploit of the Tencent app flaw to deploy GrayRabbit malware (BleepingComputer) serves as a stark reminder of the importance of staying vigilant in our daily work. It's essential to stay up-to-date with the latest vulnerabilities and patches to prevent such incidents.

**🔧 Patch Priority:** F5 BIG-IP APM devices due to deployment of Linux rootkit, as this vulnerability has already been exploited by attackers.

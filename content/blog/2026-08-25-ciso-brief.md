The increasing threat of compromised data centers is a major concern for organizations with operations in New Zealand. According to an Infrastructure Report, the country's digital backbone is at risk due to outdated infrastructure and lack of investment in cybersecurity measures. This poses significant business risks, including reputational damage and financial losses, as well as regulatory implications under the New Zealand Government's Data Protection Act.

A CISO should assess their organization's data center resilience and consider implementing robust security controls, such as multi-factor authentication, network segmentation, and intrusion detection systems. Regularly reviewing vendor risk assessments is also crucial to ensure that third-party providers meet stringent cybersecurity standards. Moreover, organizations should prioritize regular backups and disaster recovery planning to minimize the impact of a potential attack.

The lack of effective cybersecurity measures in data centers can have severe consequences for New Zealand's economy and national security.

**Boardroom Takeaway:** An independent review of data center cybersecurity is urgently required to mitigate potential risks.

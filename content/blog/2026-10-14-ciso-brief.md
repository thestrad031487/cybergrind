Third-party systems leave supermarkets vulnerable to cyber attacks. This highlights the risks of outsourcing business functions, such as supply chain management or inventory control, and how these third-party relationships can introduce new attack vectors. The French supermarket hack shows that a sophisticated attack can cause significant physical damage, including fires and spoiled food.

Regulatory implications arise from this incident, as it raises questions about data protection and cybersecurity in the food retail sector. France's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) may be relevant here, particularly if personal data was compromised during the attack.

A CISO should review their organization's third-party relationships, assessing the security controls of these partners and ensuring they meet adequate standards for data protection and access management. This could involve implementing due diligence protocols to vet new vendors or reviewing contracts with existing ones to ensure they align with industry best practices.

The impact on business operations and customer trust must also be considered, as this type of incident can have far-reaching consequences for a company's reputation and bottom line.

**Boardroom Takeaway:** CISOs should prioritize risk assessment and due diligence in third-party relationships to prevent similar vulnerabilities.

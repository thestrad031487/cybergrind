## From the Trenches

As a cybersecurity practitioner, I've been seeing some disturbing trends lately that warrant attention from organizations across various sectors. Take, for instance, the recent Adobe Commerce PolyShell Attacks, which have breached hundreds of stores due to a Brotli exploit. This is not just a case of a simple web vulnerability; it's a prime example of how attackers are leveraging legitimate technologies to launch sophisticated attacks.

Another worrying development is the emergence of hackers impersonating IT support via Microsoft Teams chat. This tactic can be particularly insidious, as users may unwittingly grant access to their systems or divulge sensitive information in response to what they believe is a legitimate support request. As practitioners, it's essential that we educate our clients on these types of threats and encourage them to exercise extreme caution when interacting with unsolicited chat messages.

In light of these incidents, I'd like to emphasize the importance of keeping software up-to-date and patching vulnerabilities promptly. This is especially crucial for organizations that rely on third-party tools and services, as seen in the case of Convertr analytics script being hijacked to steal payment card data.

**🔧 Patch Priority:** Adobe Commerce PolyShell must be patched immediately to prevent further exploitation of the Brotli bug.

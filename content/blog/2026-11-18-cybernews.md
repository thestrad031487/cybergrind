## From the Trenches

As a cybersecurity practitioner, I'm seeing two trends that demand my attention right now. First, Google has confirmed that autonomous cyberattacks are a real threat, and it's only a matter of time before they become more prevalent. This means we need to be prepared to defend against attacks that can adapt and evolve on their own.

The implications of this development are significant. It's no longer just about patching vulnerabilities or implementing security measures – we need to think about how our defenses will hold up against an adversary that can learn, adapt, and improve its tactics in real-time. This is a wake-up call for organizations to review their incident response plans and consider the potential consequences of an autonomous attack.

Another pressing concern is the growing use of AI in security operations centers (SOCs). While AI has the potential to enhance security capabilities, it also introduces new risks if not implemented correctly. As security leaders, we need to be aware of how AI can be used to improve our defenses, but also how to mitigate its weaknesses.

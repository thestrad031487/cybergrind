The US Congress has introduced the "Digital Economy Act" which includes provisions aimed at regulating cybersecurity regulations for cloud computing. This legislation would require cloud providers to meet specific security standards and provide notice in the event of a data breach. A CISO should monitor this development closely, as it could impact the company's ability to store sensitive data in the cloud, potentially resulting in increased costs and decreased competitiveness.

A recent analysis by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) highlights the need for improved cybersecurity frameworks and standards for the AI industry. As more organizations adopt artificial intelligence and machine learning, they will need to ensure that their security measures can keep pace with these emerging technologies. A CISO should review NIST guidelines and adjust internal policies accordingly.

The European Union's proposed "Digital Services Act" aims to improve online safety and security by requiring digital services to implement robust security measures and notify users of data breaches within 72 hours. This legislation could have significant implications for organizations operating in the EU, particularly those with a large online presence. A CISO should stay informed about this development and assess its potential impact on the company's operations.

The rise of "Supply Chain Chaos" has been identified as a growing threat to cybersecurity, with malicious actors targeting third-party vendors and service providers to gain access to an organization's network. A CISO should prioritize vetting and monitoring third-party vendors, ensuring that they have robust security controls in place to prevent these types of attacks.

**Boardroom Takeaway:** Organizations must prioritize the development and implementation of robust cybersecurity frameworks to address emerging threats such as AI-powered attacks and supply chain vulnerabilities.

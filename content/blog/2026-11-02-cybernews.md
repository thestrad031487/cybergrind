## From the Trenches

As a cybersecurity practitioner, I'm seeing two trends that are sending red flags my way. The first is the exploitation of critical vulnerabilities in popular software. Take Ivanti Connect Secure, for example. U.S. CISA has added multiple flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, and attackers have already found ways to chain these vulnerabilities together to gain unauthenticated root access. This is a stark reminder that even seemingly secure systems can be breached if left unfixed.

The second trend I'm watching is the use of AI-powered attacks against digital infrastructure. While some might view AI as a utopian force for good, the examples we're seeing right now suggest otherwise. Chinese hackers have built PHP2SHELL exploits and used them to breach US government-affiliated firewalls, while other attackers are exploiting vulnerabilities in Drupal Commerce Core. These types of attacks highlight the need for a more nuanced approach to cybersecurity that takes into account the evolving threat landscape.

As a practitioner, I've seen firsthand how quickly these types of vulnerabilities can spread and be exploited. It's imperative that organizations prioritize patching and vulnerability management to stay ahead of attackers. **🔧 Patch Priority:** Ivanti Connect Secure is now on my radar due to its multiple known exploits and the potential for attackers to chain them together for unauthorized access.

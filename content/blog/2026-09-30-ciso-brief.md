Malware, a virus, a Trojan, and a worm are types of malicious software that can cause significant harm to an organization's systems and data. A cybersecurity framework should include policies for incident response, threat intelligence, and vendor risk management to mitigate the impact of such threats. The CISO should also ensure that all employees are aware of the risks associated with these types of attacks and provide regular training on how to identify and report suspicious activity.

A lack of transparency in the use of artificial intelligence (AI) for cybersecurity purposes can lead to significant regulatory issues. The ShinyHunters case, where the company refused to publish FBI data, highlights the need for clear guidelines on the use of AI-powered tools in threat hunting. The CISO should ensure that all AI-powered tools are used in accordance with relevant regulations and industry standards.

The recent announcement by Citrix about two worrying NetScaler RCE zero-days exploited in attacks is a wake-up call for organizations to review their vendor risk management policies. The CISO should conduct a thorough assessment of all vendors providing services or products to the organization, ensuring that they are compliant with security best practices and have adequate mitigation measures in place.

The increasing use of AI-powered hacking tools poses a significant threat to organizations' sensitive data. According to a recent article by The Verge, hospitals and banks are not adequately prepared to handle such threats. The CISO should ensure that the organization has implemented robust security controls, including advanced threat detection and incident response capabilities, to protect against these types of attacks.

**Boardroom Takeaway:** Organizations must prioritize transparency in their use of AI-powered tools for cybersecurity purposes to avoid significant regulatory issues.

Paper audits are necessary when China-made parts are embedded in election machines because this situation highlights the risk of foreign-made, unverified components being used in critical infrastructure like voting systems. This could potentially lead to supply chain vulnerabilities and compromise the integrity of our elections. A board-level takeaway should be that we need to establish a rigorous audit process for all third-party vendors, including those providing electronic hardware.

Regulatory implications are still unclear, but it's essential that we monitor developments closely. In the meantime, CISOs should push for more transparency from vendors about their supply chains and components used in products. This might involve requesting documentation or certification from reputable organizations.

Another relevant story is CrowdStrike's post-Mythos surge. While this may seem like a tactical victory for security software companies, it also highlights the ongoing threat landscape. The blast-radius test concept suggests that even seemingly secure systems can be breached if they have vulnerabilities in other components. As such, CISOs need to ensure all systems have robust patch management and monitoring processes.

Lastly, Visa's expanded support for its clients navigating the new AI era of cybersecurity is worth noting because it reflects the rapidly evolving threat landscape. While AI-generated scripts pose a significant risk to industrial control systems, this does not necessarily mean that all companies should adopt AI-powered security solutions without careful consideration. CISOs need to carefully evaluate these solutions and implement robust controls.

**Boardroom Takeaway:** We need to establish a rigorous audit process for all third-party vendors, including those providing electronic hardware, to mitigate the risk of foreign-made components being used in critical infrastructure like voting systems.

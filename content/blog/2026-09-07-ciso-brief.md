US Unpredictability Is Giving Its Asian Allies New Reasons to Cooperate. The recent diplomatic push by the Biden administration to re-engage with Southeast Asia is concerning for cybersecurity practitioners, as it could lead to increased tensions and a higher likelihood of cyber conflicts. The US has historically taken a hardline stance on cybersecurity issues, which can sometimes be misinterpreted by its allies as a threat. As a result, many Asian countries are taking steps to improve their own cybersecurity capabilities, including developing their own national security strategies and partnering with China.

This shift in the balance of power between the US and China has significant implications for global cybersecurity. The US will need to reassess its approach to engagement and consider the benefits of cooperation over confrontation. This could involve sharing intelligence, best practices, and resources to address common threats, rather than competing against each other.

CISOs should stay informed about this development and prepare for potential implications for their organization's global operations. They may need to engage in more frequent diplomatic efforts with local partners and stakeholders to ensure a coordinated response to emerging risks.

**Boardroom Takeaway:** The US government's unpredictability is giving its Asian allies new reasons to cooperate, which could lead to increased tensions and cyber conflicts that require CISOs to reassess their approach to global operations.
